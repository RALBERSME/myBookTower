Read the text out loud to me:



I, Egon Breitschneider, must record what took place within the walls of this university library morning. Not out of pride. I must bear witness to what I came to understand there.

The worn wooden staircase leading up to the book tower was as steep as a ladder. For days, the heat had been pooling in the shaft, turning the ascent into an incubator. With every step, it grew harder to force the air from my lungs. Down in the reading room, I had really only been looking for a distraction—anything to drive the images of her from my mind. The apartment had been so damn empty ever since she packed her bags. But restlessness drove me upward, to a floor I had never stepped foot on before. Between the tightly packed reference volumes, it smelled of musty dust. As I ran my fingers across the dusty spines, gray little clouds swirled up. No one had browsed here in ages. But restlessness drove me upward, to a floor I had never stepped foot on before. Between the tightly packed reference volumes, it smelled of musty dust. As I ran my fingers across the dusty spines, gray little clouds swirled up. No one had browsed here in ages. An involuntary grin caught my lips.

The aisle was narrow. An old fluorescent tube flickered on the ceiling, but the light came from behind — my own shadow fell across the titles. I pressed my right shoulder against the shelf, my gaze fixed on the opposite wall of books. My eyes darted back and forth, searching. Which one first?

At the very far end, a particularly massive volume caught my eye. A dark blue cover shimmered beneath the grime. I reached for it, pulled it out a few inches, and blew hard across the top edge. That was a mistake. The particles exploded directly into my face and rained down upon me. My nose burned. The tickle became unbearable until the irritation released in a violent sneeze that kicked up the next cloud of dust. I turned my head away, coughing and rubbing the dirt from my eyes, but never lost sight of the heavy book.

Gripping the casing with both hands, I pulled it completely free and let the pages fall open in the middle.

An icy draft brushed my bare forearms, crept up my neck, and seized my entire body. Suddenly, the ground vanished beneath my feet. It felt as though a colossal magnet had grabbed me and yanked me forward — right into the pages. Darkness enveloped me. Bright specks zipped past. No, I correct myself as I write: the specks stood still. It was I who was hurtling forward at a breakneck speed. Then I understood. I was racing through outer space, past glowing spheres and stars that blurred into mere shreds of light in the motion.

Headfirst, I shot like an arrow through the blackness. The pressure wave ruthlessly pinned my limbs to my body. The rushing wind froze my facial features; my arms and legs stiffened to ice. I wanted to close my eyes, but out of sheer terror of the unknown, I didn't dare.

Acid rose up my esophagus. The acrid taste reached my tongue. I clenched my teeth, fighting the urge to gag, but my jaw gave way to the pressure. My cheeks puffed out until my breakfast erupted from me. The brownish-gray mass did not fall downward, however. It shot past me in wide, arching ribbons. My suspicion was confirmed: I wasn't flying in a straight line; I was rotating in a gigantic orbit. No wonder my stomach was rebelling. The carousel and I had never been friends at the fairground, either.

I had to break this rotation to survive. So I braced myself against the wind, wanting to lift my arms, but the invisible weight was too heavy. My feet responded better. Despite the headwind, my ankles could still turn. I spread my thighs, pressed my feet outward with all my might, and used my lower body as a rudder against the vacuum. My gaze was fixed rigidly behind me. I only felt the change in flight path when my right foot struck something hard with a heavy impact. In the next moment, I hit the sand face-first without braking.

Fine dust quivered all around me. I rubbed it between my fingers — white star-sand. A shooting star had caught me. Painfully, I scrambled up until I was sitting upright, and listened. It remained dead silent. The icy wind was gone. While the universe continued to rotate around me, not a breath stirred on this chunk of rock. My numb limbs slowly grew warm again. The air was cool, but bearable. My heart hammered against my ribs, but quieted down beat by beat. I shook the sand from my clothes, closed my eyes for a moment, and took a deep breath. When I raised my eyes again, I saw the naked truth: the place had nothing to offer. All around me stretched nothing but an endless, empty desert.

I crawled forward on my knees until the bare stone beneath me ended and the blackness began. Pressed flat on my stomach, I slid my hands over the ridge and craned my neck.

Slightly below me, the Earth turned in slow motion. Predominantly in shades of blue and brown. I oriented myself by the topographical features, focusing on a familiar city, a specific neighborhood. Space seemed to bend. The more rigidly I stared, the closer the surface moved. Houses grew upward, streets cut through the mass. Suddenly, I was standing virtually in the middle of the sidewalk outside my old apartment.

There she was. My ex-girlfriend.

She wore the same beige coat as the day she packed her bags. She was standing at the traffic light, arguing with a man — her new partner. I saw the familiar, defiant crease on her brow. But what left me breathless was the white cardboard sign anchored rock-solid to her head. In a glaring lipstick-red, the word blazed: EGO.

A jolt shot through my chest. It was everywhere. I looked further, saw a husband in a kitchen screaming at his wife because he was looking for the mustard; saw an infuriated father raging at the edge of a sports field — they all wore these signs upon their skulls. An invisible bond of suffering linked them. Anyone could see another’s lettering; only the bearer remained blind to it.

I must deliver them, the thought flashed through my mind. I see through it all. I am the only one who understands the pattern.

A manic savior complex seized me. Hectic, I searched my trouser pockets until my fingers brushed against the small eraser I had tucked away while drawing yesterday. I pinched the tiny piece between thumb and forefinger, extended my arm, and aimed deep downward, straight at my ex-girlfriend's head. I would rub out her inscription. I would heal them all.

In doing so, I leaned so far over the edge of the shooting star that I nearly lost my balance. In that exact instant, a reddish solar flare erupted through space. The glistening light reflected off the glassy star-sand directly beneath me. For the fraction of a second, I saw my own face on the ground — and above my brow, the blurred beginning of a giant, white cardboard sign.

The shock of this vague reflection paralyzed me. Am I also wearing…? Before I could finish the thought, the sheer terror sapped all my strength. My fingers went limp. The eraser slipped from my hand and plummeted into the blackness. The physical shock made me lose my footing completely; I tumbled headfirst off the ridge of the shooting star and fell. In the darkness, I slammed virtually into a massive, invisible barrier. A piercing pain shot up my spine, and then my vision turned black.

When I opened my eyes, I was lying on the wooden floorboards of the book tower. The neon tube flickered above me. The small of my back burned like fire. The thick blue book was gone without a trace. Where it had stood just moments ago, nothing but emptiness gaped. I pulled myself up by the shelf and just stood there, trembling. This was no dream. That vague reflection, the signs — there was a truth here that I had to capture before it faded.

I hurried down the steep wooden stairs into the reading room, rushed to my seat, and tore open my notepad. The ballpoint pen flew across the paper. Word for word, I scribbled down the report you are reading now.

I can feel the details of the vision already blurring in my mind. But the red word on those signs is still vividly etched before my eyes. Those few letters that encapsulate the entire misery of mankind. I am certain that the reflection in space was an illusion. I had the clarity. I was the savior who had recognized the pattern. I am the one who will enlighten the world.

A pungent smell of burning strikes my nose. Gray plumes crawl across the oak wood toward me, blinding my view of the paper. The smoke billows directly from the book tower; the first flames are breaking out.

The fire eats its way through the aisles; the heat rolls into the hall. I have to get out of here. I rip the page from the pad to save the truth and hastily sign my name as a witness beneath the text. My hand is shaking so violently that the final letters become utterly illegible in the thick smoke.

I stare at the soot-blackened paper. Only the first four letters of my name stand there, razor-sharp and flawless.
